Hamza » 18-March-2007 3:06pm
That's interesting, is this a printed source ? This time was very troublesome. Several military parties were disputing Khurasan, but Khurasan is may be not so important when we look to Khwarazm. This time is also the very start of the Samanids as dynasty. Nasr b. Ahmad was already de facto ruler of Transoxania (Isma'il b. Ahmad soon became the first de jure ruler).
Nasr b. Ahmad was appointed governor of Transoxania in 263 AH and Khwarazm was most likely linked to Transoxania at this point in time. So I would say Ahmad b. Muhammad b. Talha was a Samanid governor. But Tahirid or Samanid is not important in this case, more important is the person itself. I looked through several older works, but did not find a matching coin.

Dear charm, Album mentions that this group of coins is usually poorly struck. This coin here is quite well struck, but one side is poorly preserved. From the style I would say this is rather 3rd than 4th century. I see nothing that really points to Khwarazm, I don't say it's not, but Sistan (see before) is well possible too.
Most important, the inscription of this coin is typical for a governor within the administration of the Abbasid empire (this includes all dynasties that were de jure Abbasid governors). This is a fact that should be observed.
