Tiesenhausen » 12-February-2012 4:42am
There is at least one other example of this issue on zeno, 40231.

I've been working with these and others like them today, and I have an idea. The inscription is completely Arabic, ja'iz bi-dirham. It's analogous to the other Khurasan and Central Asian fulus with sittin or some other number per dirham, but in this case the minter tried to get one copper coin accepted as a dirham. Maybe that's why the issue is so heavy. Maybe also its lack of success explains its rarity. Or maybe it comes from a very small town that didn't need a lot of coinage. We can make up all kinds of stories about it, but looking at the other example, ja'iz bi-dirham seems very probable.

Tiesenhausen » 27-November-2016 10:03pm
We should consolidate all discussion of this issue under this example, to keep the ideas together.

There is now a third example on zeno, 175296. In response to an inquiry about that coin, I clarified my reading above (12/2/2012) to add that the word ja'iz seems sure, but the other word may or may not be bi-dirham (it takes some imagination to see the "bi-"; look at 40231). The word could be simply "dirham." Someone in reference to 40231 suggested Muhammad; one can see why. Or it may be the name of some other person or a place.

In the discussion of 40231, it was said that the issue is commonly found in Chach, but other commentators disagreed. The new example 175296 was found in Bukhara.

We need to look at the issue's fabric (cast, somewhat large and heavy), epigraphy (somewhat crude), decoration (circle of dots around both sides), and other features to find perhaps analogous issues that can be placed or dated. Meanwhile, all must agree that it is an Arabic Muslim issue that ought to be moved to the same category as other eastern coppers of the 8th-century Umayyad and Abbasid Mashriq. ‘Abbasid, 132-656 » Copper coinage » Transoxiana (Ma Wara’ al-Nahr) would be the best category, even if the issue could be a little earlier.

We have been sent a very nice example that will appear in our Auction 46 (May 2023). The obverse is very clear, and yes, it is Arabic ( كجنده ), which I interpret as Khujanda, which is just south or southeast of Chach. The Arabic on the other side is clear ja'iz ("current"). I would suggest a tentative dating to the period circa 730-770 (AH110-150), before the introduction of standard Umayyad fulus in the region (first dates are AH149 for al-Shash and 150 for Ferghana).
