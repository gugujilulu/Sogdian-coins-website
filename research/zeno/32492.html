osroes » 5-February-2009 10:36pm
I'd like to add some details to the conversation about the cross interpretation. First I would note there are a couple other examples of this variety published in Vainberg, on plate XXIV, images of Г VI a and Г VIь. Like the example in question, both of the coins in Vainberg have these four triangular dashes ("cross") behind the diadem tie. Other varieties have three dots behind the diadem tie (ГI, plate XXVI-ГIII-2nd and 3rd examples). In others, the three dots line up next to the last dot on the diadem to create a four dot configuration (plate XXIII- ГIII -both examples) similar to the "cross" in layout, but with round dots instead of triangles. Another example has the four round dots independent of the diadem (plate XXIV-ГIV). Then we have the coins in question, which simply turn the dots into triangles. It's worth noting that the change from round dots to triangular was accompanied by an overall stylistic change.

To put it more simply, in Vainberg's chronological arrangement of types, we see a progression in the representation of the diadem tie from three dots (ГI) to three or four dots (ГIII) to four dots (ГIV) to four triangles (ГV). (at the same time, the majority of examples of ГV and ГVI lack the dots/triangles altogether).

My conclusion from this would be that the "cross" is really just a stylized knot in the diadem. There is, however, a clue that suggests that the choice to stylize the diadem knot in this particular way could perhaps be significant: the reverse of Vainberg's plate XXIV-ГIVь also has this four triangle device floating freely behind the horseman on the reverse. This position seems to be a place where engravers at this time put important symbols, as Vainberg illustrates a coin with an Arabic inscription in this position (plate XXIV, last coin. Several more Arabic inscribed varieties are listed on the chart on VII.) From the picture of the coin in question here, it appears this piece may also have the reverse "cross," just somewhat obscured striking weakness.

ECIN Associates:
Central Asia, Khwarezm, Afrighid dynasty, King Sawshafan, ca 751-762 AD. AR Drachm (26mm, 2.60g). Portrait right, wearing Khwarezmian headdress; name in Sogdian to right, Nestorian cross to left, O above / Horseman galloping right, tamgha to left, Khwarezmian legend "M`RY MLK` sy`wrspn" around.

Cf. Vainberg GV; Rtvelazde 49; Mitchiner, The Early Coinage of Central Asia, 71 - all without cross. All coins of Khwarezm are extremely rare and silver being especially rare. This piece with the Nestorian cross behind portrait is the only known specimen - an unusual variety that is currently unpublished and unique. This coin is the subject of much discussion and debate on zeno.ru (#32492). What does the Nestorian cross mean?

The Afrighid dynasty existed from late 6th century AD to 995 AD. Many areas in central Asia used Sassanian or Sassanian-style drachms for their currency. The Khwarezmians instead used drachms of their own design, which portray the king wearing a turban-like crown on the obverse and the 'Khwarezmian Horseman' on the reverse. Some people propose that the horseman depicted on the back is the mythological founder of the dynasty, Siyawush.
