Comments by Aleksander Naymark:
     The reason I said that this is a fake was not the crude style and "unreadability" of legend (except for one seriously distorted aleph on the left, all other letters in the reverse inscription are unrecognizable). Images and legends on the imitations of Asbar struck in the first half of the 6th century are often distorted to this extent. The problem is the stuff in front of the ruler's head. Neither original coins of Asbar, nor any imitations I have seen in my life, have inscriptions in front of the face. And this coin has something absolutely unreadable there. It was hard to see in the photo.
     , however, after seeing the uploaded video, I would agree with Vld68 that this might be an overstrike and the stuff in front of the face is a residue of underlying design. Is it necessarily Nakhshab coins with leontomachia (these are indeed often overstruck by the Bukharan mint)? Maybe it is, but I cannot say for sure on the basis of the video and photo. In other words, I would like to withdraw my initial opinion as a mistake. It is likely to be a genuine late imitation of Asbar overstruck on some earlier type.
