KHWARAZMIAN KINGS. Sawshafan. Mid 8th Century AD. AR Drachm (2.05 gm). Crowned head of King right / King on horseback right, holding bow. Rtveladze 49, Zeimal pg. 262, 6. Near VF, encrustation. ($400)
Many areas in central Asia used Sasanian or Sasanian-style drachms for their currency. The Khwarazmians instead used drachms of their own design which portray the king wearing a turban-like crown on the obverse and the 'Khwarazmian Horseman' on the reverse.
REMARK:
There is a two-line arabic legend behind the rider, "Dhú-l...". Coins are known with title Dhú-l-yaminayn, i.e. Táhir b. al-Husayn (cf. ZENO #20661); or ”Dhú-l-riyásatayn”, which refers to al-Ma'mún's wazír al-Fadl bin Sahl, who was murdered/executed in 202 AH. (cf. coin with name of al-Fadl #23805). Metrologically are Khwarizmian coins with Arabic names equal/close to half of Sasanian drahm or dirham tabarí.

The pre-islamic khwarezmian Ar coins developed under a long time from imitational Eukratides-tetradrachms, with the weight reducing. The first Afrigid dyn. coins (Ramik, about AD 600) had a weight of 6,5 g, at the time of AzkatswarII it was reduced to about 2 g. As far as I know, the metrology of these coins had no connection with the tabari dirhems.
