As for Mervin's comment, July 2005, I honestly don't remember having seen this particular coin before I heard a paper about it given by Aleksandr Naymark with Luke Treadwell as joint author at the Third Kochnev Symposium at Hofstra University, 10/4/2011. The abstract will be in the Journal of the Oriental Numismatic Society this year. Naymark and Treadwell read the date correctly 160 Hijra and the mint name as Istikhan, where the Samarqand Ikhshids had resided since the invasion of Qutayba b. Muslim. This was at the height of the al-Muqanna' revolt, and they would like to think that a representative of the old dynasty issued the coin at Istikhan seeing the Muslims driven out of Samarqand.

T. & N. note that the Sogdian side should be rotated so the two prongs of the tamgha point to the left.

Anyone seeing this coin might jump to the conclusion that the number is "fulus per dirham" from other similar pieces, including some with 160 per dirham. That was my first reaction when I saw the image (again?) at the Hofstra conference, but at a second look, it is very clearly inscribed "sanah."

Michael Bates
