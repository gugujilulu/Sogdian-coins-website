Sogdian Christian text written in Estrangelo, discovered at Turpan, 9th—11th century.

Here shows the bottom half of a page (n 493 recto) in slightly modified Syriac script (Estrangelo) and Christian Sogdian language. The text, a translation from an identified Syriac original, contains a dialogue between a younger monk who poses questions and an older monk who gives answers; it belongs to the Apophthegmata Patrum. The speakers are indicated in the text by the words written in red ptry (›father‹ for the older monk) and br’t (›brother‹ for the younger monk). The page belongs to the large Sogdian codex C 2 and was edited by N. Sims-Williams (BTT XII: Folio 61 R on p. 129).

Thank you, I agree with you.
Unfortunately, both great scholars passed away some time ago.
I've consulted with leading expert in Soghdian script Pavel Lurje:
"Sogdian writing since the 6th century at least (Bughut inscription) is vertical. The earlier specimens found in the Upper Indus valley show that they were comfortable with virtually any orientation of the lines on the rock. Following the established Near Eastern and Iranian tradition, the majority of modern scholars (with some notable exceptions) prefer to read Sogdian in horizontal orientation from right to left."
His opinion about the legend on the coin is expressed at the second specimen.
