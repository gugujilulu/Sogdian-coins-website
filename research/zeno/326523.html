Photo Details
Interesting specimen of the coinage of ruler Tudun (土屯王), with the reverse of the coin undergoing some sort of striking error that allowed for what seems to be an inverted concave image of the obverse to be struck instead of the tamgha. The obverse seems to also have been struck with some degree of error, resulting in a very large rim space and unordinarily small bust area. Very special specimen and is the very kind gift of a close friend. Graded by ZDGS, a Chinese numismatic grading agency with specialty in early medieval Sogdian coinage. Grading number 1121011500095. Personal collection item.

Dear Charm,
ZDGS is a relatively new coin grading agency founded in 2019, and please forgive me for the English what I meant to say was "with a specialty" not "specialising in". The reason for this is they hired a numismatist by the name of Mr. Lang Rui, who co-wrote the first catalog of 7-8th century Sogdian and Semirech'e numismatics in China back in 2018. The book's name is called 《昭武遗珍：唐安西都护府地区货币研究》, translating to "The Treasures of Sogdia: A Research of Coinage under Tang Era Anxi Protectorate", and was published as a part of the GBCA (the oldest coin grading agency in China) numismatics literature series in 2018. Mr. Lang Rui is the head of the early Medieval Central Asian coinage grading team at ZDGS - the only other agency with a specialised team on this time period and region, as far as I know, is GBCA's Xinjiang Regional Grading Institute. So I consider ZDGS to have a specialty, in comparison to other Chinese agencies, in Sogdian coinage.
