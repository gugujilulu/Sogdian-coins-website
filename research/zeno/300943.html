This is not really an imitation, but a late specimen from a Sogdian coinage that started in the middle of the 1st century CE by reforming one of the lines of Antiochus imitations. As I haven't yet published my typology of Antiochus imitations and related coinages (culpa mea!), there is no up to dae typological arrangement I can refer here. The results of my investigation into the matter, however, are reflected in the scheme of the development of Sogdian coinages in my recent article on Ashtat (илл. 1):
The Sogdian coinage which this specimen represents started with the reworking of the terminal type of final stage in the development of Antiochus imitations, Line 1. The initial type of the series had Sogdian legend in front of ruler's face, but with the degradation of the type it was soon blundered and eventually dissappeared altogether. Most likely this series continued being struck until the middle of the 2nd century CE. The majority of the few currently registered finds come from the area west of Samarqand (Ishtikhan, Kosh-Rabat).
