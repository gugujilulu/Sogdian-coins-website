Photo Details
AR coins of Phseigaharis (φςειγαχαρις) are probably one of the most rare among all early Soghdian coinage.
Not more than 10 pieces were reported, as I can judge.
Obv.: Portrait of king, with typical for Saka-YuehChi iconography. On this specimen even plume on headdress is visible.
Rev.: Standing Hercules, leaning on knotty cudgel in his right arm and bearing lion-skin in his left one. Nimbus or wreath around his head. Greek legend in square script.

Dear all:

I have only photos of the six examples in Mitchiner's Indo-Greek and Indo-Scythian Coinage, Volume 4 (no. 513); one example in his Early Coinage of Central Asia (no. 94 - same as one of the Vol. 4 examples); one in ACW (no. 2833); one in Rtveladze (no. 20, page 96); Alram's Iranisches Personennamenbuch (Tafel 40, no. 1262); and in Senior's Indo-Scythian Coins and History, Volume II (page 214; A7.1).

The style of the bust on the above example doesn't match any of these (although the Rtveladze coin is quite crude); the engraving of the lettering looks 'wrong'; the almost perfect circle impressed by the reverse die is -- maybe not unheard of, but, at minimum, unusual. And now that I've seen the "before" scan -- I worry more because I've seen the same patina (include the "green patches") on some Hunnic forgeries a few years back.

I may be wrong, and happy to admit I am. I'll lighten my initial response: Could be an ancient imitation of different style; could be some sort of modern reproduction.

And while they are usually lumped in with the coins of Sapalbizes, Arseiles and Pabes (because of the Hercules reverse), the style of the obverse bust is closest to the soldier and horse issues (bare-headed) of 'Hyrcodes' -- if that's the correct rendering of the legend. Thought the Phseigacharis coins are found in and around around Bamiyan?

Dear tkmallon, as I could understand, this coin’s authenticity puts you in doubt due to:
1. Details of iconography, letters and common manner of the coin.
2. The same patina as you saw on modern fakes.

Hmm.. about first point I have to tell that before this one I saw only one genuine coin (also with south Soghd origin) and picture of three specimens from British Museum, published by E. V. Rtveladze’s in his “Ancient and Early Medieval Coins of Historical and Cultural Regions of Uzbekistan”, in article, dedicated specially to Phseigaharis (p.176). Those three, as I can judge, much more crude than even Zeno #35294 (which was in my arms some years ago).
First, Rtveladze mentions 8 specimen at whole (5 in BM, 1 in Punjab Museum, 1 in History Museum in Tashkent, 1 in the State Hermitage in St. Petersburg) and accentuates no one has exact place of finding known.
Then, he wrote that Mitchiner (his work is out of my access) considered possible to tie up place of issue of Phseigaharis coins with Bamian region due to:
a) The image of Hercules copies the image of him on coins of Kujula Kadphises,
b) Square Greek script was not in use to the north of Hindukush up to Phseigaharis time, while in Kabulistan it was already on late Hermaios’ silver issues.
So, we can see Mitchiner’s only mediate argumentation of possible circulation area. (I have to repeat, I can rest upon only Rtveladze’s interpretation of Mitchiner).

But Rtveladze himself shows that the image of Hercules on Phseigaharis coins is the closest to his image on North Bactria Demetrios-imitations and to the same on early Soghd coins. Also the square script was used on Sapadbizes coins, whose domain included the capital region of Bactria – Balkh and Shibergan oasises, as well as south part of north-east Bactria.

Rtveladze’s proposal was the Phseigaharis’ domain could lie somewhere between west and east parts of South Bactria, somewhere on modern Afghanistan Samangan province.

About your second reason for your doubts... I would propose with regret that you obviously came across much more fakes than I did with genuine ones, that is the reason of difference between our approaches. You compare details and common appearance of unresearched coins with previously seen fakes, interpreting your hesitation to the side of hesitation, when I compare them with previously seen real ones interpreting my hesitation to the side of hope… Both approaches have the right for existence, I guess, and I thank you for your interest to this coin and for your doubts, because I feel the problem of Phseigaharis domain is worth the discussion.

But about exactly this specimen I can add only one statement. The coin is as genuine as today's sunrise, because it was unearthed in my presence, in circumstances excluding any other assumptions, together with specific for such epoch ceramics and so on. In the south Soghd area. I can’t add more for the moment, thank you for understanding.

In soon, I am going to post here another Saka-YuehChi coin of unknown type, found out not far from this one.
