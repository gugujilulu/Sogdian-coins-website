Photo Details
Original description by Laosa:
Kai-yuan Tong-bao, strange calligraphic variety.
This is a Central Asian imitation of Tang Dynasty square-holed coins with the legend ‘Kai-yuan Tong-bao’ (開元通寶).
Another possible reading is 周元通寶 [Zhou-yuan tong bao].
Unearthed on the territory of Kyrgyzstan.
Image source: www.amurklad.org

Character 開 [kai] is written with the radical similar to 口 instead of 井 between the “doors” (門), it also resembles the character 周,
but I suppose it must be a degraded character 開 [kai].
Character 元 [yuan] below the hole is written in a strange archaic script and has both ‘left and right shoulders’ (左右挑双挑元).

The similar coin unearthed in the same area can be seen there: Zeno # 172880.

Also compare to Zeno ## 162023 and 162024.

Dear charm, I have already explained how to prove it.
If we will accept your Idea and you will place all known coins found in Semirech'e in chronological order,
we will see one strange peculiarity: those who imitated chinese coins, picked up only early kai-yuan coins and zhou-yuan. It is highly unlikely, especially by taking in sight a large gap between these emissions.
Further, reading the above character as zhou 周 is also questionable, because from a calligraphical point of view the zhou 周 character is also written not perfectly. I agree, at the first glance the upper character looks more like zhou 周.
But if you will see the selection of the images of the imitative kai-yuan coins collected by Laosa, you will see how the upper character 開 was degraded in time.
Dr. Kamyshev does not called them imitative coins, he says they are "Chinese coins on the Silk Route".
But if you look carefully, you will clearly see they are of local issue. Moreover, chinese were withdrawn from Semirech'e after Talas battle (751 AD, so 200(!) years before zhou-yuan coins were first cast in China) and after that date islamic coins were in circulation. Whether the chinese cash of the Song period circulated here, or they was lost by the travelling merchants then or later - it is not known, but one is clear: there are no imitative issues of the coins contemporary to the Song period known in Semirech'e.
