With tamgha behind Herakles. Alans in Bukhara?
Uploaded by kabarna
Pre-Islamic Asia curator Håkan Fransson, Sweden

Reference: А. И. Наймарк. Подражания тетрадрахмам Евтидема с тамгой: аланы в Бухаре в I веке н. э.? [Aleksandr I. Naymark. Imitations of the tetradrachms of Euthydemus with tamgha: the Alans in Bukhara in the 1st century A. D.?] (In Russian).
Summary. In the middle of the 1st century CE, Bukharan coinage imitating tetradrachms of Greek-Bactrian king Euthydemus enters а transformative stage: (1) a Sogdian name of a Bukharan ruler and the Aramaic heterogram MR’Y representing princely title replaced the right line of the blundered Greek legend (not earlier than the middle of the 1st century CE); then, however, (2) Sogdian legend was removed, blundered Greek legend was restored, and nomadic tamgha replaced the distorted Greek monogram (last third of the 1st century CE); after that, (3) tamgha was removed and monogram restored, thus returning to the initial immobilized type; (4) title MR’Y and Sogdian name replaced the left line of the Greek legend (end of the 1st — beginning of the 2nd century CE); (5) Sogdian legend gradually squeezed out the remnants of the Greek one and became circular. This sequence of coin types allows us to suggest that sometime in 60s or 70s CE a nomadic dynasty took over the Bukharan oasis and remain in control of it for several decades. Judging by the form of the tamgha its origin could have been connected to alans-abzoi, who occupied the North-Estern coast of Caspian Sea at that time — similar tribal/clan tamghas are recorded on the walls of the sanctuary Bayte-III situated on the northern chink of Ustyurt Plato, not far from the Caspian gulf Mеrtvyi Kultuk.
