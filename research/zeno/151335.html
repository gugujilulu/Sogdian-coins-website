Peroz drachm (or imitation), with 4 or perhaps 5 countermarks.
1. At four oclock: Göbl cm 79, sogd legend in two lines "δγcy βγy".
2. At two oclock: similar legend, 79var. Not listed by Göbl, but looks somewhat different to 79. See #125612.
3. At 11 oclock: Göbl cm 82, sogd legend "tkyn", tegin.
4. At 10 oclock and partly under cm 3: parts of unknown legend?
5. At 6 oclock: Göbl cm 83, sogd legend.
As far as I understand, no arabic countermarks. The coin belongs to North Tokharestan.

The coin appears to me to be an official Peroz drachm, based upon the consistent style and clear mint mark (AT). I think that the low weight is a result of clipping, which is common for these type 3 Peroz drachms with Sogdian countermarks.
Peroz was captured by the Hepthalites and the conditions of the ransom meant that large numbers of newly minted silver drachms were sent by donkey-train to the Hepthalites, where they circulated, were presumably countermarked in this fashion, and were subsequently imitated as Gobl Em 287.
Though evidence is somewhat circumstantial, I would place the coin here:
