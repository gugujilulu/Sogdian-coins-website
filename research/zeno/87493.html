Comments by V.P.Lebedev:
Few years ago at one of Bulghar sites in Ulianovsk area among kufic dirhams (whole and broken) was found this drachm of the last Khwarizmshah of the Afrigid dynasty Azkaswar II. Comments by V.P.Lebedev:
At Zeno Islamic name of Azkaswar II and his reign period are represented according to Vainberg’s book of 1977 – Abdallah, 770-800 A.D. Despite the absence of publications with different opinion, during 30 years after issue of the book, now there are some facts which contradicts to this point of view.
1. Reign period.
In 1950 in Damascus was found large hoard of 3815 coins, mainly – Umayyad dirhams. The oldest coin of the hoard is drachm of Khusraw I (559-560). Youngest coins - 73 dirhams of 131 A.H./748-749 A.D. In this hoard was 1 specimen of Khwarezmian drachm, type ГVIa. The hoard was published in Damaskus in 1972 and recently commented in details in the article by V.Kuleshov (Proceedings of the State Hermitage, XLVIII, SPb, 2009, p.86-97). This fact, by Kuleshov’s opinion, proves the issue of drachms type ГVIa not later than 712 A.D., and ruler struck these coins is Azkajwar b. Sabri.
2. Islamic name of ruler
Islamic name on the drachm type ГVIб – Abdallah – was read by Vainberg at unique drachm, found in the Bolshe-Tarhan tomb (Tatarstan). In the first publication of this drachm (Henning, Khalikov 1964) and in the Vainberg’s publication of 1977 drachm was not described in details and no image was published. S.A.Yanina, who described in 1964 3 coins from the Bolshe-Tarhan tomb, at the Khwarezmian drachm has read name Muhammad on the obverse, but the name in front of shah’s face has not read!

In 1977 Vainberg first time published the drawing of the Arabic name and its reading (with reference to Yanina!) - Abdallah.
But presented drawing can’t be read as Abdallah, which should be written only with ayn. All other Islamic names which were read on drachms, are written without mistakes – Muhammad, al-Fadl, Jafar, Khalid.

In the rare Islamic Coins (1950), p.10, Number 35. George Miles gives a description of a coin of the similar type in the collection of ANS. That particular coins bears the name in arabic Kufi of Al-Fadl, which is either al-Fadl.b Yahya al-Barmaki or Abu l-Abbas Fadl b. Suleyman al-Tusi. It's not that important, but Miles quotes the works of Tolstov, particularly, "Monety Shakhov Drevnego Khorezma i Drevnekhorezmiiskii Alfavit"in Vestnik Drevnei Istorii, No,4, 1938, pp.120-145. It was Tolstov, who tentatively read the Khwarizmian legend as ' Abdullah Shah".
It's interesting, though, that in other book of s. Tolstov "Drevny Khorezm",Moscow, 1948, he gives variants of the coin with names of Fadl, al-Fadl and Ja'far, but doesn't provide such a reading as a "Abdullah Shah" for the ruler at all.

Comments by Dr.V.Nastich: The difference is because of the pronunciation of the letter ض [d.ad] which sounds as emphatic [d.] in Arabic and as simple [z] in Persian. The same is valid for 'ramadan'/'ramazan' and a lot of other words containing the cited letter.

Dear hfrans, you wrote: "That particular coins bears the name in arabic Kufi of Al-Fadl, which is either al-Fadl.b Yahya al-Barmaki or Abu l-Abbas Fadl b. Suleyman al-Tusi."

It's difficult to be sure, as the coins are rare and the good provenance still should be provided, as I understand. I am quoting the position of Miles, who cites Dr. Tolstov, the highest authority on Khwarizm in the middle of XX century. Unfortunately I don't know the later research on this subject.
Tolstov believes that it was Barmakid, as Fadl b. Yahya was a Provincial governor up to 179 otr 180 AH. It's also logical, as there were coins with name of Ja'far [b. Muhamad], who was an earlier Abbasid governor and some versions with al-Ghitrif b. 'Ata, who was a governor of Khurasan immediately before al-Fadl b. Yahya.
