There is an article about the Bukharkuda dirhams in "Supplement to ONS Journal 193", by Luke Treadwell. He, as earlier Walker, reads the second part of the legend "...mimma amara bi-hi al-amin ´ali (ála [yaday]?) sulayman lillah". Walker dated the coins to AH 193-195, but Treadwell argues for an earlier date (AH176?-193).
He writes: "... it is likely, as Walker noted, that the beginning of regular dirham production (AH 193) coincided with the end of the Bukharkhuda series. Therefore it seems reasonable to assume that Type 10 (above type) was struck during the reign of Amin´s father, Harun, possibly as early as 176 AH, when Amin´s title first appears on dirhams of al-Muhammadiya."

That as a rule such high-standard coins were not used in daily life and were a subject of accumulation and accounts at the large bargains, term of their manipulation should be considerably increased.

I believe the inscription on these coins is to be read

bism Allah Muhammad rasul Allah muhammadiyya mimma amara bihi al-Amir `Ali, Sulayman lillah

`Ali would be `Ali b. `Isa b. Mahan, governor of Khurasan and the East. He governed 180-91 Hijra, but his initial status was subordinate. The coins with his name are likely to be from his later years, when he governed directly under the caliph, after the fall of the Barmakids.

Sulayman might well be Sulayman b. Hamid al-Azdi, who governed Samarqand in 190 (Tabari III, 708; trans. vol. XXX, 260).

If this variety is attributable to `Ali, are there any other varieties to be attributed to al-Amin?
