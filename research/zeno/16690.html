CENTRAL ASIA - SOGHD - BUKHARA

Abbasid coinage of Bukhara with the name of Caliph al Mahdi (770-785 AD).
("Bukharahuda" type).

AR Dirham. 3.10 gm. With the Arabic name "al Mahdi" behind the head. Top of the crown is crescent shaped with a dot. Three large dots below the head.

Luke Treadwell, in his article about the circulation of Bukhar Khuda dirhems in Samanid times (Sup to ONS Journal 193), writes:"Type 3 (al-Mahdi) includes two subtypes, both bearing the title al-Mahdi, the first with three pellets on the bust and the second, less common type with the exclamation bakh, bakh (bravo) in place of the three pellets. Walker believed they were struck from 163-166 AH, during the period when Musayyab b Zuhayr was governor of Khurasan, However since the hoard evidence demonstrates that the first sub-type was by far the most common type of the Bukharkhuda series (see table 3 below), it is reasonable to assume that it was struck over a much longer period than that proposed by Walker, probably throughtout al-Mahdi´s reign and perhaps from as early as 151 AH, the first year in which his title appears alone on the copper fulus of Transoxania."
No mentioning of imitations...
These coins circulated even into the Qarakhanid times, but what evidence is there of an imitation coinage, even of a posthumous coinage?
