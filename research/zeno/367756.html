I saw these images (MACW 3348-49) about 25 years ago, but never seen a "real" coin, until now. Mitchiner used the collections of British Museum when he organised his catalogues. Probably these two coins are in BM. Maybe there is some data about findspots or other origins, that made Mitchiner attribute them to Choresmia. He also calls the rev tamgha "a Choresmian tamgha".
It is alo listed by Senior, A19.5, together with the first Choresmian tetradrachm (A19.5T).
I don't know any more, but I find this interesting... And I can't find a similar tamgha somewhere else.

Comments by Aleksander Naymark:
I have to admit, I do not know what is it. It is certainly not Khorezm, as none of the early coin series attributed to Khorezm by Mitchiner were ever minted there. There are two lines of Eucratides imitations in Norhtern Tokharistan (North of Amu-darya/Oxus), but this specimen belongs to neither of them. There is a line of Sogdian imitations of Tokharistanian imtitations of the oboli of Eucratides in the western part of Samarqand Sogd, but this specimen has nothing to do with them. There are lines of Eucratides imitations in the spurs of Hindukush, but for them you would need to consult somebody who is better acquainted with Bactrian materials.
