CNG, Inc., Triton IX, 9 January 2006; Lot 1138:
KHWARAZMIA. Artav (Artabanos). Circa 1st-2nd century AD. AR Tetradrachm (13.81 g, 1h). Bearded bust right, wearing cap-like headdress with posterior flap; behind, Nike flying right, crowning king with laurel wreath held in his right hand / IUIVEWIE MELUV EILVILV (blundered Greek legend), king on horseback right; tamgha behind. B.I. Vainberg, Monety drevnego Khorezma (Moscow, 1977), pg. 109, 7 = CAAU 307; S.P. Tolstov, "The Coins of the Shahs of Ancient Khwarizm and the Ancient Khwarizmi Alphabet," Vestnik drevney Istorii (Moscow, 1938), pl. VI, 1 = R.B. Whitehead, " Notes on the Indo-Greeks, Part II," NC 1947, pg. 38, fig. 3 = Göbl, Dokumente, pl. V = MIG Type 498. Good VF, light scratches in reverse field. Extremely rare, the seventh known specimen.

Coins of the kingdom of Khwarazmia from the ancient era are extremely rare, and consist of only a handful of examples. The coinage begins with barbarous imitations of tetradrachms of Eukratides I of Baktria, but in the last century BC, the Khwarazmians develop their own types, assimilating characteristics of Baktrian, Parthian, and Indo-Skythian prototypes. On the coins of Artav, the Eukratides/Heraios prototype tetradrachm has a bust similar to the Parthian style with Nike crowning the king, and a Skythian-like tamgha on the reverse that serves as a symbol of all Khwarazmian coins until their last issue in the 8th or 9th century AD. The bust also has purely native characteristics, such as the cap-like headdress. Truly a wonderful example of the ancient coinage of central Asia.

I worry about comments like that of gazali that this coin might be a forgery. I am not an expert in ancient coins of Khwarizm, so I am unable to state an opinion about the coin. I think if someone suspects a coin is a fake, they should explain why they think so. What about the quality of the die engraving suggests it is a fake? Why does vld68 think the coin is genuine? It is too easy to make unsubstantiated accusations of forgeries on open lists like ZENO that can harm the reputation of people who own or trade coins. I am certainly in favor of exposing people who manufacture or trade in modern fakes, but ascertaining whether a coin is a forgery is not always easy to do, especially if you only have a photograph. Any claims of forgery should be supported by actual evidence.

And it prompts me that the surface of letters and details of portrait on coins are not plain, they seems unevenly arised above the coins field, unevely convex. Beside that the line bordering upper surface fex letters is lowering to the field smoothly, blurred and not so sharp as we can see on those coins. The surface also doesn't look naturally for coins been lying in earth more 1500 years, even in very dry desert climate, and cleaned after uneatrhing.
Then, we have at least 3 images of Artaw coins in database about which exists some hesitations and not only mine on two of them. It seems to be notable fact for coins which amount is known as handful of examples... #20866 is called modern fake, about #16558 we have charm's comment: "Seems that this coin, as well as #20866, is a
modern forgery. I suppose that with high probability it came from the same source that fakes at the current Elsen's auction #86." About the third we have emotional reaction of some venerable and respected members on my proposal.
I would like offer anybody to repeat my experiment. I saved all 3 images of above mentioned coins, opened them in Adobe Photoshop, changed transparency and adjusted
colour a little. After superimposing layers on each other, one by one, I noticed that the outlines of highest places on all three coins (which should be the most concave on
die) coincide. My conclusion was that all 3 are struck by one die which was engraved in minor details from one attempt to another. That was I meant saying about engraver's skill... I know that it was common practice in ancient times to engrave details again on abraded dies. But why we recognize the specimens of one pair of dies fake and genuine differently ?
It would be interesting to hear opposite arguments pro authenticity.
