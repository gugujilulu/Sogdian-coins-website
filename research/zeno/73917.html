Dear hfrans, about tamgha's name, I've just translated the local numismatists used name for it, it is rather unexact one. Yes, it differs from Paykend’s “dancing man” tamgha, but both names are relative.

Then, this name - Bukharkhudat - for the type in general - is described as relative and unexact, already many years ago. In Russian numismatic literature those coins were often called “so called Bukharkhudat”, or even “bukharkhudatoide”, stressing relative nature of the name.

The only type of early coins, most probably struck in western Soghd is one with Bukharan countermark (without exact dating, and I do hesitate they were struck in Varahsha, I already proposed, they should be of Vardanzi origin, in the north of Bukhara oasis, where ruled separate Vardan Khudat dynasty), when much more numerous types originated from Samarqand. And we can not say which were first. BTW, some early types have origin from Merw. I do not know other mints, and I am not sure about Chach as mint place (if so, it could be only already after Arab conquest).

I think, in such situation you can agree with me, the type in general will be more acceptable to eyes in Soghd category, isn’t it?

Some small notes on the Bukhar Khudat coinage (with a sogdian "Bukhar Khuda" legend):

Prototypes: 1. Sasanians, from Merv. Tom Mallon suggests theses are imitations?
2. (Hephtalite?) imitations of the Sasanian issues from Merv.
These both types were often countermarked with a Bukharan tamgha.

According to Narshakhi: “at the time of Abu Bakr (AD 632-634), the Bukhar Khudat of Kan was the first to mint silver coins in Bukhara”.
The sogdian legend is interpreted as "Bukhar Khudat", Lord of Bukhara (see Walker: A Catalogue of the Arab-Sasanian Coins). If this interpretation is correct, the early issues of this type was minted for/by the rulers of Bukhara, probably in Bukhara and/or somewhere in the Bukhara oasis.

Zeimal writes: “Here (in Bukhara) the earliest imitations of Sasanian drachms minted by Bahram V, often called Bukhar Khuda-coins, were minted”

A. Naymark, 2004, in “Stage one – The Palace of Khunak”, describes coins with a long sogdian legend, interpreted as “Bukharan Great King Khunak”, probably the same person as the Vardan Khuda, who usurped the Bukharan throne, ca AD 690-707.

Zeimal also writes: “From the second half of the seventh century/beginning of the eight century, Bukhar Khuda type coins were issued not only in Bukhara but also in several other regions of Central Asia, especially Samarqand. A detailed typological analysis allowed E. A. Davidovich to distinguish two Bukhar Khuda series, one minted in Samarqand and the other in Chach (Ilak)”

It has been suggested that the Mug drachms might have been minted in Samarqand.

Luke Treadwll, in ONS 193, 2007, supplement, writes about the circulation of the Bukhar Khuda dirhems at the time of Ibn Fadlan (AH 310) and Ibn Hawqal (ca AH 300):
“…The fact that ghitrifi and muhammadi dirhems were still in circulation at that period shows that Ismaíl´s dirham issue had made little impact on the monetary stock of Bukhara. The reason for this is not hard to find: although several other mints in Transoxania and Khurasan (principally Samarqand, Shash. Balkh and Andaraba) produced Samanid dirhams in quantities, Bukhara itself produced no silver coinage until the mid-330s. Furthermore, Bukhara produced little copper coinage before the beginning of the 4th/10th century. Thus, there was a real need for local coinage in Bukhara and it was this gap that was filled by the ghitrifi dirham…”
“…monetary circulation in that city (Samarqand) followed a different pattern. There the Ismaíli dirham formed the backbone of the currency…There too, Bukhar Khuda dirhams played a role, albeit a minor one. Ibn Hawqal tells us that the muhammadi dirham which circulated in Samarqand was “min nuqud bukhara” (of the coinage of Bukhara), a clear reference to the fact that this coinage originated in Bukhara and not in Samarqand…”
“…To sum up, the testimony of these two authors, both eye-witnesses to life in Samanid Transoxania, reveals two important conclusions about Bukhar Khuda dirhams in the 4th/10th century. First, that their use was mainly restricted to the city of Bukhara. Second, that they were a low denomination currency…”

Dear gazali, I don´t read Russian. Above are some extracts from articles available in a western language. For me, the conclusions must be, that the Bukhar Khuda coinage was a Bukharan currency, in the 8th century spread to other parts of Sogd, but mainly circulated in Bukhara even in Samanid times! So, no reason to list them in the Sogd category!

The Turgar drachms/dirhems differ from the Bukhar Khudat types, they lack the Bukhar Khuda legend. They might be listed in Samarqand – Turgar (or cross-linked).

Maybe this is all out-of-date. If other theories/facts are known in Russia, I would be most interested!
