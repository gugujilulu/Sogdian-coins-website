Photo Details
Interesting Kaiyuan with crescent on reverse, which is usual on Chinese Kaiyuans but uncommon on those from the Semirech'e. Possibly casted at Suyab by the Turgesh (?) though this is speculative without further evidence. Some sort of casting error occurred to make the reverse of this coin concaved and obverse convex. GBCA graded: 1115933492. Personal collection item.

The upward crescent is common in later Kai Yuan coinage
The scyphate shape seems unusual in Kai Yuan but I find them often in Wu Zhu coinage that preceded this era, I think it may be connected the the use of "mounds" on the "ao pan liang" from even earlier. The zao bian =cut down wu zhu are often scyhate from the process of cutting, but even not cut down wu zhu coins are sometimes this shape.
The dark color of this coin may suggest copper rather that bronze metal, which may go along with your idea of a "Silk Road" mint, how about Kuche which had a copper mine?

Dear ChinaNerd,
Thanks for your knowledge on the scyphate shape! While the upward crescent is common on Tang-casted Kaiyuans it is far less common on re-casts that are produced in the Anxi Protectorate or the Semirech'e, hence why I left a comment wondering whether a subcategory for crescents should be added under the Semirech'e category.

Kuche could be a possible mine, considering it was the headquarters of the Anxi Protectorate intermittently throughout the 7-8th centuries, before the Anxi was engulfed. Given the similarity between this Kaiyuan and the Tang Kaiyuan's design Anxi seems probable, especially knowing that it was forced to self-cast for decades. At the same time, I wonder if this could also be a early type of re-cast by the Turgesh, given that the scyphate shape is quite rare among Anxi's self casts, and possibly shows a different casting technique as you've mentioned.

In any case IMHO because of the weight difference, cast difference, and visible calligraphic differences (particularly with the character "元") I do believe this is a cast from the historically termed "Xiyu" (西域) region, though whether it is under the Anxi Protectorate or a polity in the Semirech'e is unclear at the moment.

Dear YansuLu
There exist these concave/convex coins but some have intentionally pressed shape
Early casting have this cast into the design - my friend calls them "fat meat" surface.
Zeno 9506 is ban liang with this idea cast into the surface. Later Qin types have this on obverse and reverse- The Ban liang That I posted seem to be missing but can be seen in the Gratzer/Fishman "The First Round Coins of China" book A11.65 and others.
Wu Zhu (late Eastern Han types seem to be the ones with the repoussé) that I have been playing with lately have occasional scyphate coins but also some that have the field pressed out just below and above the hole and occasionally just above or below. This can not be explained by it being a consequence of cleaning the center hole. I haven't worked with Tang Dynasty for a while but this might be interesting for me to see if this process continued into this late of coinage. I don't recall any Sung Dynasty having this either.
