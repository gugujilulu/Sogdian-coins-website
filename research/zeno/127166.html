But irrespective of that division, one must unify the headings for different postings, so that they don't look like mixed fruit in the oriental bazaar - one starting with either a ruler's personal name or office title, another with the name of a dynasty, the next with a subdynastic appellation, then date, metal, title(s) again... {though most of them are already explicit in the upper-level categories}, absolutely ignoring that all of those would automatically stand in alphabetic order. I certainly agree with orientexpert, 110%. Coins are issued by mints, not rulers, and the mint is the mint of the city even if a governor has the power to appoint the master. All Khwarazm coins should be under Khwarazm.

As for spelling, at the ANS and still I used the Encyclopedia of Islam spelling as much as possible (almost always), ignoring two letters they use (K with dot and Dj) for the Anglo-Saxon equivalents Q and J. EI is not the only standard, but there *must* be a standard, otherwise. as orientexpert points out, if everyone spells Khwarazm as he/she feels like, how do we find all the coins of that country? I use Khwarazm because EI uses Khwarazm. I used to use Khwarizm but changed when I discovered I was out of concordance with EI, and only for that reason.

And how come, hfrans, "Moh" and "Jabrail"? Maybe those are right in some book, but if we don't write Muhammad *always" and Jibra'il *always*, how can we search?

Bosworth's Dynasties is also a reliable standard for Arabic names in English, and best of all is the Index, volume 40 of Tabari's translation in English (but only for the first three centuries).

There are good standard references for French, German, Russian--but we have to choose one if we want to be consistent.

It's a lot of work, however, to enforce standard format and spelling. We can live with inconsistency if necessary. At least, let us each and every one try to be consistent.

Orientexpert's simple, rational and usable sequence, mint, date, metal (+ denomination *if necessary*), then names on the coin, ownership of the coin, and publication (incl. zeno) is what I use and recommend. Dynasty is not useful, dynasties were not nations or governments.
