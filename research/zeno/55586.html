Mervin » 19-March-2008 3:18am
Tom, the coin does not look cast to me, though it is of course hard to say without the coin in hand. I have a coin with a similar countermark in my possession which is clearly not cast; I have uploaded it as #55630. In any case being cast would not necessarily explain its low weight, and it would be technically difficult to cast it so thin.

A perusal of SNS shows that coins bearing this type of countermark are all well-worn, and some have small flans suggesting clipping. They are on the whole lighter weight than typical uncountermarked specimens.

For whatever it is worth, Michiner associates this type of countermark with the tribute money paid to the Hepthalites in connection with the captivity of Peroz. In any case they may have seen extensive circulation, wear and possible clipping in a specific region.

hfrans:
By a strange coincidence, I got this week, from a friend, a small script, "The Voice of the Turtle" (North American Journal of Numismatics), Vol V, No 12, December 1966.
On page 365-366 is an article "Three countermarked sassanid coins" by Raymond J. Herbert. It describes three Peroz (or imitations) with cm:s of Göbl 79 type. Two (C and D) looks like the ordinary type, but cm E very much resembles this two line cm. Mr Herbert concludes "...C and D are variants of the same countermark, while E, although superficially similar, seems to be different."
